\section{Reproducing the seed-777
run}\label{reproducing-the-seed-777-run}

The cell isolates training stochasticity: identical data, identical
config, identical evaluation, with only the training seed changed. One
structural caveat, from your own released
\texttt{train\_worldmodel\_logs.txt}: your run was not one continuous
process. It ran fresh to epoch 300 and was then resumed to 500 in a
second invocation, and since the pipeline saves only model state, that
resume restarted the optimizer moments and re-seeded the RNG stream at
epoch 300. My 777 run is a single continuous process, so the comparison
is strictly seed-plus-run-structure. This means a couple of things: A fresh,
continuous seed-12345 rerun will not bit-reproduce your released
checkpoints after epoch 300, so divergence there is expected rather than
a failed reproduction. And your collapse onset (peak at 310) sits just
after the restart point, which suggests a control worth running: seed
12345 as one continuous process, to separate the restart from the seed.

\textbf{1. Training.} One practical trap on the unmodified code:
\texttt{train\_models.py} auto-resumes from the newest
\texttt{checkpoints/world\_model\_*.pt} (setting the start epoch from
the filename), and failing that it silently warm-starts from a repo-root
\texttt{world\_model.pt} at epoch 0, which is the worse failure. Move
aside both \texttt{world\_model.pt} and the \texttt{checkpoints/}
directory first (the shipped \texttt{wm\_checkpoints/} folder is not
read), then:

\begin{verbatim}
python train_models.py --phase world_model --config config.yaml --seed 777
\end{verbatim}

500 epochs, checkpoint every 5, exactly your config (\(\sim 42\) s/epoch
on an L40S in my run, from the checkpoint timestamps). On my branch the equivalent is
\texttt{-\/-fresh\ -\/-checkpoint\_dir\ ckpt\_f1rs\ -\/-seed\ 777}.

\textbf{2. MPC sweep.} Your own \texttt{eval\_mpc.py} sweep driver,
unchanged (it runs \texttt{wm\_mpc\_policy.py} per checkpoint): 20
episodes per checkpoint, base evaluation seed 12345, all 100
checkpoints, which are exactly its defaults. About 12 h wall-clock on an
L40S in my run.

\textbf{3. Metric sweep.} Three full sweeps with seeds 12345, 1, 2, each
run start-to-finish in a \textbf{single process}
(Section~\ref{the-rng-stream-position-effect} explains why this
matters), dynamics Jacobians off (they do not enter CROF), then
average per checkpoint. 

\textbf{4. Analysis.} Centred (not trailing) 7-point moving average over
per-checkpoint MPC means; Spearman of seed-averaged
\texttt{jac\_rof\_combined} against the smoothed curve. My analysis
script (\texttt{analyze\_llc.py} on the branch) prints the curve
characterisation, the full correlation table, and the composite
checkpoint picks. You're welcome to it.

\section{The RNG stream-position
effect}\label{the-rng-stream-position-effect}

At epoch 5, my L40S run of your unmodified pipeline reproduces your logged
metric values exactly. The pipeline is bit-deterministic given the same seed, device class, library
versions, and RNG stream position. At epochs 150/280/310/450, my CUDA
and CPU values agree with each other to \(\le 0.002\) but sit 5--10\%
above your log (e.g.~epoch 280: 0.206 against your 0.186). The reason is
stream position, not numerics: your sweep computed all 100 checkpoints
in one process, so the RNG stream had advanced by mid-sweep, whereas
fresh single-checkpoint runs re-seed. Epoch 5 is the only stream-aligned
comparison, and there everything matches exactly.

This means that your logged per-checkpoint values are one
consistent stream realisation, reproducible by re-running the full
sweep in order. And per-checkpoint Jacobian metrics carry a draw-to-draw
sd of about 0.003 (\(\approx 4.6\%\) of the metric's 100-checkpoint
range in your run), which is why I average three independent
single-process sweeps. The same protocol is what I'd suggest for the 777
reproduction.

\section{Scripted-data experiments}\label{scripted-data-experiments}

\textbf{Collector.} A scripted pilot around Gymnasium's built-in
heuristic with per-episode corruption modes: clean; noisy (action noise
\(\sigma \sim U[0.4, 0.9]\)); blackout (engines cut from
\(t_0 \sim U(20, 200)\)); disoriented (\(x\) and \(v_x\) sign-flipped as
the pilot sees them, from \(t_0 \sim U(0, 80)\)). Mode probabilities
were calibrated against your human dataset's good/bad/middle return mix
only. Discrete version: probabilities 0.26/0.08/0.12/0.54, train 750
episodes / 151,907 steps (mix 43.1/35.2/21.7 against your
41.7/41.5/16.8), val 122 / 24,217, seeds 22345/65432. The collector
script is on the branch.

\textbf{Result.} Your untouched discrete pipeline on this data: peak
smoothed MPC \(-43.2\) at epoch 110 (raw best \(+4.3\)), final-10 mean
\(-156.3\), genuine rise-peak-collapse (span, the smoothed curve's peak
minus its minimum: 119.5, against 111 on your run). So at fixed architecture and matched return mix, data
provenance moves planning competence by \(\sim 200\) points: human
\(+153\), scripted discrete \(-43\), scripted continuous \(-392\).

\textbf{Correlations on this run, relevant to your U-shape question.}
This run collapses, but ROF is not U-shaped in the useful sense: raw
jac\_rof\_combined has its minimum at epoch 5, the two pool components
point in opposite directions (jac\_rof \(+0.603\), jac\_rof\_bad
\(-0.385\)), and the combined metric lands at \(\rho_s = +0.186\) with
its checkpoint pick about 110 points below the oracle checkpoint.
Meanwhile val\_loss outperforms the structural composite here:
\(\rho_s = -0.434\), turning at epoch 115, within about 6 points of the
oracle. So on this evidence the U-shape and the collapse can
dissociate, and a U-shaped-ROF collapse detector would miss this
collapse while validation loss catches it. (One more lead from your own
run, for what it's worth: your ROF minimum sits at epoch 280 and your
MPC peak at 310, so in the one collapsing run where ROF does work, its
turn precedes the performance turn by about 30 epochs.)

\section{The continuous-action leg,
briefly}\label{the-continuous-action-leg-briefly}

For completeness, since your limitations section asks about other
environments: I ported the pipeline to LunarLanderContinuous (action
codec + Gaussian CEM at your planning budget: horizon 25, population
384, elites 48, 4 iterations, \(\alpha = 0.7\), \(\gamma = 0.97\)).
Scripted continuous dataset: mode probabilities 0.13/0.15/0.22/0.50,
train 750 / 187,627 steps, val 122 / 29,650, seeds 12345/54321;
reward-Markovianity gate confirmed comparable to your task
(\(R^2(o,a) = 0.215\); your protocol reimplemented, and your discrete
dataset gives 0.235 under the same reimplementation).

The MPC never worked at any checkpoint (best smoothed \(-391.7\)).
Diagnosis on the epoch-60 checkpoint: the model is excellent on-manifold
(imagined vs real 25-step discounted return under ground-truth actions:
correlation \(+0.87\), mean gap \(+1.2\)), but CEM shows an optimism gap
of \(\approx +50\) (imagines \(+10\) to \(+15\), realises \(-5\) to
\(-58\)). Random shooting at matched budget finds the same exploited
optimum (identical returns and gap), and the environment is solvable
through the same loop (heuristic pilot \(+288\) to \(+299\)). Done-head
gating did nothing, a prior-std uncertainty penalty made it worse, and
CEM warm-starting made it much worse (\(-534\) vs \(-275\) at matched
seeds). Classic continuous-action model exploitation on narrow data, in
other words, and a caution for anyone porting the benchmark to
continuous actions with scripted demonstrations.
